Understanding the challenges faced by students in today's academic environment.

What Causes Pressure?

  • Academic/Systemic Shock: Scoring poorly in a mock test, a sudden drop in grades, or facing sudden instability like national exam paper leaks and cancellations.
  • Institutional Harassment: Experiencing caste-based discrimination, linguistic exclusion, or systemic bullying by peers or professors.
  • Financial Terror: Realising that delayed government scholarships or high-interest private tuition loans are putting their family under severe financial strain.
  • Thwarted Belongingness: The feeling of being completely alone, isolated, and disconnected from family, friends, or the student community.
  • Perceived Burdenedness: The agonizing belief that their existence, financial needs, or academic failures are causing immense pain and financial ruin to their parents.

Total Cases of Suicide by Student

Student suicides in India have climbed to unprecedented crisis levels, with the latest comprehensive National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB) data revealing that 14,488 students died by suicide in 2024. This represents an 80% surge over the last decade from 8,068 deaths in 2014, making students one of the fastest-growing vulnerable demographics in the country. States like Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, and Tamil Nadu report the highest absolute numbers.

Reasons for These Suicides

  • Academic Stress & Fear of Failure: Rigid attendance policies, heavy curriculum demands, and intense societal and familial pressure to succeed create an environment where failure is heavily stigmatised.
  • Institutional Neglect & Discrimination: A nationwide survey by a Supreme Court-appointed panel revealed that 15% of students in Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) felt severely distressed, and 9% reported suicidal ideation.
  • Exam Instability and Systemic Failures: Repeated entrance exam disruptions and paper-leak controversies create intense uncertainty and fear for students preparing for years.
  • Fear of Failure & Parental Expectations: Rigid cultural definitions of success tie a student's self-worth entirely to academic performance.

Government Viewpoint

The Government of India views student suicide as a critical public health crisis and a manifestation of severe mental and emotional distress rather than an individual failure. This perspective is codified in the Mental Healthcare Act, 2017, which officially decriminalised suicide under Section 115, shifting the focus from punishment to mandated care and rehabilitation.

To address this crisis, the government, in coordination with the Supreme Court of India, has implemented a multi-pronged prevention framework across schools, coaching hubs, and universities.

  • Legal accountability: Supreme Court directives mandate institutional responsibility and immediate action for student distress.
  • Mandatory resource action: Institutions must resolve scholarship delays and faculty shortages to reduce stress and exclusion.
  • Immediate referral: Campuses must provide medical support and connect distressed students to professional care.

How Can We Help?

As a Parent

Parents are the closest to the children yet the farthest from their pain. Daily conversations, emotional support, and realistic expectations can help children feel safe, valued, and heard. Parents should avoid comparison, understand their mental health, and seek professional help when needed.

As a Student

Students should learn to ask for help without shame, maintain healthy routines, and build a support network. Academic performance does not define self-worth, and reaching out early can prevent long-term suffering.

As a Friend

Friends can notice subtle changes in behavior, start supportive conversations, and encourage students to get help before the situation becomes overwhelming.

We Must Listen, Support, and Act

Student well-being is not a side issue. It is the foundation of a healthier future.
